TALKING MECCA
Conversations at The State Mecca bingo Hall,Kilburn. June 1999.



PAT, a player.
In conversation with Karen Stripp and Justine Marley.



JUSTINE: ........the idea was that we were trying to create apparition-type things, so it’s not like you go there and you know you’re going to see something....

PAT: Taken by surprise....

JUSTINE: Exactly. You’d go "Ooh! what’s that?"

PAT: Yeah......there are a few people I’d do that to, but......not actually things appearing......

(Laughter)

JUSTINE: Anyone you’d care to mention?

PAT: No!

JUSTINE: Ok......I can’t even ask you any of these questions, because you haven’t seen them...... "have you spoken to us before?"

PAT: Oh, yes.

JUSTINE: "Have you spoken about the images to other people?" ......not applicable.

PAT: I’m ever so sorry about this!

KAREN: That’s alright!

PAT: I will go and have a look later......

JUSTINE: Well, I’m shocked!

PAT: And I don’t go down the bar, so.........once I’m in that seat at the ledge at the stairs, there, that’s where I stay, you know. I’m a lot of good, am’nt I?

JUSTINE: Have you been told anything about the images, or about the project, by the staff, or the other players?

PAT: No. I just read the leaflet last night, that’s all. I read your pink one last night.

JUSTINE: Ok, so you know that we’re trying to get a booklet together? 

PAT: Yes.

JUSTINE: Right, so is there anything that you would like to record, or be remembered about  The State, in the book?  Is there anything that you have a memory of that you think would be a good......?

PAT: Only something my friend told me. Her dad actually  helped ....I don’t know did she say built it  , or decorated it .......before the war, or something, you know.....

KAREN: Right, in the thirties?

PAT: And it was never ever touched, sort of thing, during the war, which was amazing, you know.

JUSTINE: Was it open during the War?

PAT: I think it was still acinema, yeah.

JUSTINE: D’you know what your friend’s name is?

PAT: Sheila. 

JUSTINE: That’s a stupid question, isn’t it? 

PAT: I think I know her! (Laughs)

JUSTINE: Do you think she’d mind if we used her name?

PAT: No, I’m sure she wouldn’t mind. She used to be a supervisor here, but she’s left, she’s retired now. She fell and broke her back and she had to retire early. But she’s alright now, thank God. It  could have been a lot worse.

JUSTINE: Is there anything that you would like recorded or remembered about your life as aBingo player?  Because you’ve been playing for quite a long time, haven’t you?

PAT: Hmm.....just that I didn’t win enough times, probably - that’s my memory of it.

KAREN: Are there any particular little stories or anechdotes that we could put in, that you wouldn’t mind........?

PAT: Well.....this is years ago, when it was Top Rank, sort of thing. You used to.....I think it was full houses, or something......and we used to play for holidays. And I was really lucky in those days, we had three or four holidays, with my friend that used to work here. And one of these holidays was in the Norfolk Broads - which was a total, total disaster!  Off we went, my friend and I......

KAREN: Sheila?

PAT: Sheila. And two of her girls. One of my girls is one of her girls best friends......and this is going back years, probably they were only about sixteen at the time. And we went to the Norfolk Broads. And when we got there, there was this huge,  huge blooming cruiser thing, was lined up, and he says "This is yours" sort of thing. So we says "Will you show us what to do?" sort of thing. So he says "Well, yes" and he took us down for about  ten minutes, on the Broads, and he says " And that’s it, everything’s very easy.....you’re on your own now". Well, we started off, didn’t have a clue. We really didn’t have a clue, and we thought if we put it in neutral it would stop.....which of course it doesn’t, you haveto put it in reverse, and then..........Anyway, we really, really knocked that boat to hell. Honestly, it was really terrifying - it was bashed to hell! I suppose the safety buoys around the sides......well, we’d lost all them, we just couldn’t stop. We’d be going down, along the broads, and "Will you catch our rope for us?" and they’d be going  "Go away!". Of course, we used to try and stop, put it in neutral,and we’d just keep gliding over to the middle of the Broads again. Poor man his son was in a fishing boat one day, and er......completely over they went, because.....

(Laughter)

KAREN: What, they overturned?

PAT: Mm. It was awful! And I’ll always remember this place -  it was called Wroxham - and we were going down towards it, and there was this little olde worlde bridge - little tiny   bridge - Oh, goodness! Anyway, as we got near to the bridge, the boats from the other side were coming through, all lined up this side, and of course, we couldn’t stop, see?
And the kids had got friends - young boys on this other boat  - and we were shouting to them, as we ‘re trying to get near them "Will you catch our rope to stop us?" No, they wouldn’t, sort of thing. So, it was my fault because I was steering.....as I went to go under the bridge, the boat sort of turned sideways, and we were stuck across the way, see? And of course by this time acrowd had gathered on the bridge at the one side, and the boat had started to go through the bridge sideways, just scraping each side - scrape, scrape , scrape. And it must have been.....the bridge was not even ten feet long, and by the time we’d scraped through sideways, they’d all come over the bridge to the other side . Oh! you’ve never seen a mess like it! And we thought "What are we going to do?"Anyway, somebody grabbed us from the left hand side, so we tied up there, and we stayed in Wroxham that  night. We thought, "What are we going to do? How do we get back up again?" you know. But eventually, the next morning, we did, and one of the kids took over, and they were a lot better than us, sort of thing. And we went up and up and up and up andup........and it was Potter Higham, and, seemingly you can only go so far, and you must get  - oh, I forget his name - the pilot. He must take you through that bridge, you see?  So, we got up there, and there was lots and lots of boats parked each side of these huge houses with big gardens, and their little boats on the water.....well they were just not.......we used to look back and see them all bobbing off back down the Broads, and everything. You know, this was just like bloomin’ .....what was it called in those days?......Candid Camera, you know - someone has to be filming this. But, as we neared Potter Higham, we saw the bridge, and we thought "Ohh, we’ll never get through!". So, I can’t remember if it was me, or was it Jill, or Jackie......? One of them swerved the boat around, and the bridge was ahead, and there were lots and lots of yachts there, and all the sails down, sort of thing. Well, this man on the corner was in the process of putting his sail down....the mast down.....as we swung around, and the boat started going over towards him. And just as this mast come down, it just come straight through the front window of the boat, straight  out the other......and there was a television  as well.....very thickly, thickly quilted inside , and it went straight through the quilting.  But it didn’t go out the other end of the boat. Of course, we saw this happening, and I still see it today in slow motion. It was as if we see this huge .....I mean, masts only look that......until they’re coming towards you........and it pierced , and then it come through, and it was like a spider’s web, all in slow motion, a spider’s web where the mast come through. Well, we couldn’t go any further, it was a huge  big panoramic window at the front, you know. So, of course everybody had their cameras out, there were all these Japanese tourists, all running to the side taking pictures, and we were trying to hide and draw the curtains, you know. (All laugh)  The pilot phoned from the boatyard........that’s when it was, it was at the time of the Falklands, because  every day  we’s limp home wi’ another bit missing off the boat - and it was a whole corner missing off the front - every day there was something.....and someone would say "You’ve had a good day today. I’d like to see the other one!"  But, the pilot came, and of course he had to tie us up and we had to stay there over night because he had to send someone from base, sort of thing, to put all new windows in and everything. And then the next  morning, he said "I’ll take you out this little inlet, back into the Broads, and turn you to go up." So, we says ok then, which he did . And he took us out , and as we got to the corner he jumped off. And he says "Now turn right, and go back up the Broads". There was a big huge boat, moored just on the other side, and of course we just went straight  into that ! (laughs)  

The kids, they used to go up on the sun deck, and they used to take their pillows, and their blankets, and then come down for something to eat, forget they were up there......and we’d look back, and all the pillows and blankets were floating back .......It was lucky this holiday was completely insured by Top Rank, you know? But we eventually  made it back to where we started from......

JUSTINE: How did you manage to get back? 

(All Laugh)

PAT: They sent someone to bring us back, and he brought us back to wherever it bloomin’ was, in Norfolk somewhere. He got us into a bay, and the boat was in an awful, awful mess. And we were hauled to the office, sort of thing, and Sheila had to go, and I had to go, and he says "You’ve got to do a drawing of what happened, with the mast and everything" Well,  you’ve never seen anything so funny, sort of thing!  But he said, "I’ll come  every day, and take you out, if you want to go.....". I mean, a lot of people used to go way down to Yarmouth, out in the sea bit! I mean, we’d never have been seen again, sort of thing. So we says, "No, no.....". He says, " It’ll be no trouble, I’ll take you out any day, wherever you want to go, and I’ll do it." We says, "No, no....". So, I think, for the rest of that holiday - seven, eight, nine days - it was just moored there, and we just used to get up in the morning, get ready and go and get the bus.....and we just used to use the bus and the train from then on. A friend of mine had a caravan in Cromer, which must have been pretty  near, and I asked her to go and see what had happened, sort of thing - I give her the name of it  - Well, they went into this huge airplane hanger, and she says it was.....

KAREN: A mess.....

PAT: ....completely no good! and they asked a man, and he says "No, this is awrite- off!"sort of thing. I mean, what it must have cost ........

KAREN: And........did they stop giving away the holidays, just after your holiday, by any chance? 

PAT: Well, funnily, I never had another one. I mean, we had a good few at Butlins, and Sunshine Holidays.......I mean, they were great! But I think that was very irresponsible, you, to just give you ten minutes , and then "Here you are!"........it was just horrific. And every time I see the holiday programmes : "Have a lovely holiday on the Norfolk Broads!" I think, "I wish we’d had a video camera, because nobody would.....".....it was so horrific, and really, really terrifying! We didn’t have a clue.........and nobody would take our ropes for us.

JUSTINE: Why not?

PAT: Because they didn’t want to know us!

JUSTINE: What, because they thought you were out of control?

PAT: Yeah, they just wanted us to keep away from them, yousee. They used to go "Go away!"

JUSTINE: That was aholiday to remember.....that would be brilliant, if you could bring some photos.....are you going to here next Sunday? You know next Sunday’s the special event?

PAT: Yes, he was saying last night.

JUSTINE: Was he? Are you going to come? 

PAT: Yes, he announced it on the mike.

JUSTINE: I don’t know if we mentioned this when we spoke to you before, but when we first came to Bingo we thought it was a bit like a church, in a way......as far as the caller being like a priest, the players being like the congregation,  the scale of the buildings........do you think there’s a connection?

PAT: I don’t see it as that. Probably because it’s been so long I’ve been playing.

JUSTINE: But, it’s quite an interesting analogy, do you think?  Or do you find that a bit perverse?

PAT: Yes, I suppose.....No, no.

JUSTINE: No? Ok......if a miracle could happen within a Bingo hall, what would it be?

PAT: What, relating to Bingo?

KAREN: Whatever.Whatever you think a miracle is, or could be.

(Pause)

PAT: Golly.

(Pause)

PAT: Just that.......well, as regards Bingo, and as regards winnings........just people who really, really need it, would have nice win, and that, you know? So many people are lucky here in Bingo - the same people - and they just go "House". And there’s other people you think "Oh, I’d love to see her or I’d love to see him get anice amount of money so that they can do something with it" you know? And you never see them winning, you know, you really don’t.

KAREN: Yes, other people have said that.

JUSTINE: Yeah, somebody was saying that just now, that some people, they just go......

KAREN: They’re so calm and cool about it aren’t they?

JUSTINE: I don’t know how some people can be so contained. I’d be an excited mess!

PAT: There really are a lot of poor souls here, you know. It’d be lovely for them, it really would.

JUSTINE: We asked you if you had any lucky charms, didn’t we?

KAREN: We’ve done that - you’re the Scots piper.....you’re the one that’s got lots of Scots pipers.....

PAT: And I got another one last week.....not a Scots piper.....my grand-daughter: "Grandma, I want you to take  this becausethis is my most precious jewel." Now look what she gave me last week......

KAREN: Oh, it’s gorgeous.

JUSTINE: It’s a little light pull is it ?

PAT: I don’t know what it is. I think at one time, it was a tree decoration, or something. But she has it in her jewelry box, and she thinks it’s her most precious jewel. "I really want you to have it Grandma!"  I says, "You keep it. You love it." No, no......That was last Sunday, and I brought it with me last night, and I won ten pounds. So I must tell her that her lucky charm - her lucky jewel, as she calls it - that’s my jewel!
