TALKING MECCA
Conversations  at The State Mecca Bingo Hall, Kilburn. June 1999.




RONNIE.
In conversation with Karen Stripp and Justine Marley.





KAREN: So, have you seen any of the projections around the building at all?

RONNIE: No.

KAREN: So, how long have you been coming to Bingo?

RONNIE: I used to come when this was a cinema, and the Bingo hall was behind. And then, of course, it opened as The State - which was ‘The State Of The Art’ Bingo Hall, in all of the country, wasn’t it?

KAREN: Yes!

RONNIE: And I used to come then. Then I moved to Spain, and didn’t go at all until I came back.....came back to this area and back to The State. Then I moved to Wales for ten years, came back to this area recently, back to....

KAREN: Back to The State!

RONNIE: Yes. Funny isn’t it?

JUSTINE: Did you know about The State before it was a Bingo hall out the back, when it was a cinema?

RONNIE: Yes, I used to come when it was atheatre  - The Dominion Theatre, wasn’t it? - I saw Bill Haley  here years ago....

KAREN: Did you? 

RONNIE: Yes - dancing in the aisles!

KAREN: Somebody else said exactly the same thing, that they saw Bill Haley and they danced in the aisles....

RONNIE: Yes?

JUSTINE: Have you got any photos of that?

RONNIE: what, of Bill Haley and dancing in the aisles?

KAREN: Yes, or of The State.....

RONNIE: No.

JUSTINE: That’s amazing.....

RONNIE: It is really, isn’t it, when you think.

JUSTINE: Yeah...because this is all I know of it: how it is now.

RONNIE: Yes....I was living in Harrow at the time, and  a boyfriend......well, he wasn’t really my boyfriend, he was somebody who  wanted to be.....You could write off to the newspaper, to get tickets, and he wrote off and got three tickets in the third row for me and my mother - they did  it properly in those days, you know? They invited your mother  as well........(All laugh)

JUSTINE: Was she dancing in the aisles?

RONNIE: No...no. She didn’t go quite that far.

JUSTINE: Did he?

RONNIE: Yes.

KAREN: So...obviously it’s changed loads since you first came......

RONNIE: Yes. Well, when it was the Bingo hall at the back, there, they had a raised platform at the front with arm chairs, almost. There were rows of ordinary wooden seats, you know - the fold-up ones - but then there were actually armchairs and  it was........we used to come and sit up in the armchairs.......

JUSTINE: Did you have tables?

RONNIE: Yes, you had...like coffee tables.....it was more like being in a pub, if you were up there, otherwise  you had it on your lap, on a pad.

KAREN: Right, so they had like, little clipboard things?

RONNIE: Yes.

KAREN: So what do you think about  it now, compared to, say....when you first started coming?

RONNIE: I guess it was more fun in the early  days......

JUSTINE: Because of the novelty factor?

RONNIE: No, it wasn’t so much  the novelty. It was less.....high powered, if you like. People used to get a lot of fun out of the  ‘clickety ducks’ and all that  type of rubbish, you know.

KAREN: Yes....do you miss that? Do wish that they still did that?

RONNIE: No, not really, not now, no. I don’t wish they did it, but....the atmosphere was a lot more friendly.

KAREN: And did more people come? Was it busier?

RONNIE: It was certainly busier when this first opened, than it is nowadays. It was very, very busy. It was packed.

KAREN: And was there more of a variety of, say, age groups, or .......was it mainly women, would you have said then? Because there’s quite a lot of men that come  now......

RONNIE: No, I’d say it was the same - more women - probably about the same percentages, and young people did used to come a lot.

KAREN: But they’re coming back again now, aren’t they?

RONNIE: Yes. Well, I only come in the afternoons, basically, so it’s mostly the pensioners time.

KAREN: So how often do you normally come?

RONNIE: Couple of times a week. Sometimes  I’ll go two or three weeks without coming, it just depends.

KAREN: Yes...because some people come  really religiously, don’t they?

RONNIE: Some people come every day and every evening, and I don’t know how they can afford it, because it’s not cheap.

KAREN: It’s not cheap, I know.

RONNIE: I suppose it could be if you played with one book....

KAREN: Nobody does that!

RONNIE: No, nobody does it. It would be boring.

KAREN: So, when you first started coming, did you come because somebody  else came with you, or....?

RONNIE: No......I had two little children , my husband worked nights, I had an au pair, and I used to think "What can I do?", and I thought "I’ll try Bingo". And I came down, and within a couple of weeeks I met a group of people, we all got friendly with each other. We used to say if we all sat together we’d win, which seemed to work!

KAREN: So are you superstitious?

RONNIE: Not particularly. I don’t think so, no. But we always  did feel that if we sat together we’d win...

KAREN: You had a better chance, kind of thing.....


RONNIE: Yes.....we used to will it out, will the number out  for each other....I do that.

KAREN: If you think about it enough, the number will come?

RONNIE : Yes. 

KAREN: Of course, sometimes it does, and sometimes it doesn’t!!

RONNIE: Sometimes it does, yes.

KAREN: So, you’ve not got lucky charms, or......

RONNIE: No.

KAREN: Do you have any lucky numbers, or any kind of.....

RONNIE: Well, I once won a thousand on the number nineteen, so I like number nineteen!  (laughs) 

KAREN: Did you? So was that here, that you won the thousand?

RONNIE: Yes. You were taken into an office in case you had a heart attack.

KAREN: Really?

RONNIE: Oh, yes. You weren’t allowed to have it. You had to go in, and be counselled,  almost, for about  a couple of minutes.....

KAREN: God.....that’s amazing!

RONNIE: And then they’d say "Do you want a cab to take you home?"

KAREN: Which is very sensible....

RONNIE: Because it was a lot of money in those days.

KAREN: Well...it is now. So what did you spend your money on  -  your big winnings?

RONNIE: Er.....we took it home, if I remember rightly threw it in the air and watched it all fall down! (both laugh)

KAREN: Me and a friend won - I don’t go too much now, but I used to go quite a bit - we used to go to  the Mecca in Camden, and we won four hundred quid between us. We always split it when we go. We were like "We’re four hundredaires".....we were so excited we took it home and we threw it up in the air! (Both laugh)

RONNIE: That’s exactly what we did! Oh dear......I did something very stupid last Saturday.....it was for.....it was you talking about  four hundred.......three hundred and ninety nine pound, the last house in there. And when someone called "House", I still wanted two numbers. I knew one of the numbers was number sixty, I couldn’t remember what the other number was. But then you have to go up and queue for your Lates. So I just got up and went to queue for my late books....

KAREN: But you had two numbers left!

RONNIE: I was waiting for two, and someone called "House". So I thought I’d go and queue.....

KAREN: And it was afalse call?

RONNIE: .....it was a false call. And I thought "Shall I go back?"and I thought "No...two numbers.....not worth it.". And I stood there......and sixty came up, and I thought "what was the other number I wanted?"and there wasn’t time to run back. And they called about  four more, which obviously were indelibly printed on my mind, got my tickets, went back and I’d had one of them! I would have won it , if I’d stayed where I was and not gone back.

KAREN: I always think it’s silly when people rush out at the end as well. Always, don’t they? I never quite understand  quite why......

RONNIE: It could be a false call.

KAREN: It could be a false call, and they’re out the door!

RONNIE: Well, I could have run back, and I just thought.......if I’d been waiting for one it would have been different. But two? No......no, never. I’ll never make that mistake again!

KAREN: No! So, do you meet friends when you come here, or........?

RONNIE: No, I’ve only moved into this area a few months ago, and , apart from my daughter who still lives near,  I don’t  know many people. I’ve lived in too many different places, you know........friends everywhere......

KAREN: But you always come back here, though? 

RONNIE: Yes, I suppose it’s familiar ground. I mean, everyone keeps saying "Oh, you must try the one in Cricklewood"....and I live nearer Camden, really, because I’m in Belsize Park. I haven’t been to the one in Camden.

KAREN: It’s nice.....it’s smaller. I don’t know if it’s as nice as here. The one up the road is very different : it’s all new and very busy......really crowded. It’s good, but the chances of you winning  are much less.

RONNIE: I suppose I could try it....

KAREN: Try it. But it’s very different from here. It’s a lot, kind of, calmer here, and people know each other and stuff....

RONNIE: Yes....my ‘Bingo friend’......I mean, a lot of people don’t like Bingo.....my Bingo friend lives in Barnet , and she’s soon moving to Clacton anyway, so.....

KAREN: Right, so you’ll come with her...?

RONNIE: Well, I won’t be able to.....

KAREN: And what would you be doing if you weren’t coming to Bingo? What would you be doing today if you weren’t  here at Bingo? 

RONNIE: Writing. I’m trying to write a book.

KAREN: Are you?

RONNIE: That’s what I’d be doing. Or decorating.....something like that. All those things I should do  rather than the things I......

KAREN: And do you play anywhere else, like church Bingo, or.....nothing like that?

RONNIE: No.

KAREN: Do you do the Lottery, or......?


RONNIE: Yes, I do a couple of lines on the Lottery.

KAREN: And do you do horses or dogs or anything like that?

RONNIE: No.

KAREN: So it’s just  numbers?

RONNIE: I don’t know a thing about  horses.

KAREN: And how many Bingo books do you play at atime, normally?

RONNIE: Five. I like the number five. I had two friends over from America not so long ago, and brought them. We borrowed somebody else’s card, I think, for one of them.....I’m not sure how it worked. Anyway, she won the thousand! She didn’t say "House", she said "I got it!" (Both laugh)

KAREN: And was that the first time she’d been?

RONNIE: First and last I should think!

KAREN: That’s typical ‘Beginners luck’ isn’t it?

RONNIE: Yes!

KAREN: So...do you have any particular place where you sit, or....

RONNIE: Yes......yes, if I can.

KAREN: Why?

RONNIE: Why?  The lighting’s better on the upper bit. The lighting’s just a bit subdued down there, a little bit miserable,  so I prefer the upper bit. I’m quite near where they call for the  er....

KAREN: The Cash Bingo.

RONNIE: Yes, Cash Bingo. I like  it, I suppose because there’s no-one this side of me, and there’s aisles either side. So, you’re not worrying about  your smoke going over anybody.....

KAREN: And you can spread out a bit .....

RONNIE: Yes. I just tend to head for that seat, you know. I do find that if someone else’s in it, I tend to think "Oh, someone else is sitting in my seat!" - my seat - ! (Both Laugh)

KAREN: You get all territorial about it!

RONNIE: I mean, not aggressively, "Oh, someone’s in my seat" (Laughs).....but....it’s really stupid isn’t it?

KAREN: Yes, it’s funny. but, I mean, that’s just practicality. It’s not because you think it’s lucky or anything.

RONNIE: No!

KAREN: Because some people  think it’s lucky to sit in some seats and not in others.

RONNIE: No. If I win with a particular pen, I think "Oh god, I hope  it doesn’t run out  - it’s lucky. But of course it runs out, and you never win with it again anyway!

KAREN: Yes, so you’re not superstitious particularly?

RONNIE: I’m not terribly superstitious, no.

KAREN: And are you religious at all? What do you believe in, if anything.....?

RONNIE: Oh...I believe in something.......greater than us. Because if there’s nothing greater than us, what’s the point of everything, you know? I wouldn’t say I attend church. Oh...I believe...............do you really want to know what I believe ?

KAREN: Yes...

RONNIE: Um...........I believe in flying saucers, or UFO’s. I believe in, let’s say UFO’s.

KAREN: Yes.

ronnie: And......er........I believe that Adam and Eve wre actually deposited on this planet by a more advanced planet. And that if you translate the Bible with UFO’s in mind, and a more advanced planet with artificial insemination in mind, that it explains the history of the Earth. It explains the way that Christ disappeared  - He was being trained  in his own......umm.......I’ve read the Bible a lot, with that in mind, and it makes alot of sense to me........I can’t go through all the different details, but.........

KAREN: No, no.......but that’s interesting..........

RONNIE: But then, that doesn’t.....because I think.......in some versions of the Bible, in Genesis, they don’t just refer to The Lord, they refer to the Lords, and the Gods -  it’s in the plural, as if Adam and Eve were visited by more than one. But that doesn’t deny my belief in a spiritual God.......

KAREN: No.....it’s just different from the kind of standard.......

You know, I mean......er......the shepherds looked up and saw angels........I mean, what? You know?  And the light was so fierce they had to hide their eyes?  But we wouldn’t actually be God, if we colonised another planet,d’you see?  They would maybe think of us in their history as gods, but we wouldn’t be.

KAREN: Yes. It’s the Aztecs, isn’t it?  When the Spanish invaded, when they went to Peru, the people thought that these fair skinned .........

RONNIE: They believed they were gods, yes.........

KAREN: .........because they hadn’t seen anything like them before, you know. Just because they were so different from them, and because they came on horseback as well - so they were, kind of, big. Yes, that’s interesting.....

KAREN: Yes, I’ve heard about that.

RONNIE: Giving the contours of the World, as everybody thought for years incorrectly. But then now they’ve found out that  they  were the original contours....... they could only have made these maps from the air - and how did they? You know.

KAREN: Exactly.....there’s a lot in that........

RONNIE: There’s alot of interesting theories. I’m just  fascinated by it.

KAREN: We thought, when we first came here........it kind of occurred to us that there was something quite similar to church, about coming to Bingo. I don’t know if that sounds really mad.........

RONNIE: No. I think a lot of people, especially  in the older age group, go to church because they’re amongst company. It’s as simple as that.

KAREN: Yes. And Bingo’s similar.

RONNIE: Yes - they don’t feel alone being on your own, there’s a lot of people on their own in there. You’re not terribly concious of being on your own. I mean, I suppose I’d just as soon sit in apub and have a drink, but it’s not something I’d do on my own, yousee?

KAREN: Yes, and it’s a safer feeling here, isn’t it?

RONNIE: I suppose so. I think women are widowed earlier  than men, mostly, and there’s a limit to what you can do when you come to a certain age group, you know?
