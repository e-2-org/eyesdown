Conversations  at  The State Mecca Bingo Hall, Kilburn. June 1999.




GEORGE  the caller. 
In conversation with Karen Stripp and Anna Best.




ANNA: Have you seen any of the images  around the building?

GEORGE: Yes.

ANNA: The projections....?

GEORGE: Oh , you ain’t  going to ask me what I think of ‘em, are you?

ANNA & KAREN: Yes!

GEORGE: Oh, my God.....(laughs)

GEORGE: You know what  I think of ‘em.

ANNA: But you saw the buses.

GEORGE: Saw the buses....saw all of ‘em, I think.

ANNA: Are there any that sort of reminded you  of anything? ......reminded you of anything, or......you thought........what did you think about  them?  Come on, out with it Anna! (laughs)

KAREN: You can be honest.

GEORGE: Waste  of time, I thought  they  were. See, I’ve hurt "Kate’s " feelings  now! (laughs)

ANNA: No, I’m alright....

GEORGE: Wait  till she sees that  BEANO.......holes in her tights.....(all laugh)

ANNA: I’m going to have to get  my  revenge  on you, George...I’ll find someone  in the BEANO........Did you recognise  any of the images ?

GEORGE: What  do you mean, recognise?

ANNA: Well........(long pause. All laugh.) It doesn’t  work, that question, does it?  We’ve only just.....we’re going to be testing this out  on you .

GEORGE: I’m the first bugger , am I?

ANNA: Er.....what about the buses?  You said you  did  recognise  the buses.

GEORGE: Yes....

ANNA: You said they  all go round Marble  Arch except  the Number 11.

GEORGE: Yeah, so I thought......I couldn’t figure  where it was, ‘cos it couldn’t  have been, er....what’s the other end of the park? .....erm...Hyde Park corner - that’s down the other end of Park Lane - there  are  no shops  there ....you could see shops.....so, it couldn’t  be there, so I thought,  it must be Marble Arch, and Number 11’s gone astray, which happens sometimes, you know, they come from the garage  or something.

ANNA: Yes. Have you talked to anyone  else about.....have you had.......have you chatted  with anyone  else here about  some of the images, or has anyone  mentioned them? 

GEORGE: Not really.

ANNA: No.

KAREN: None of the members  sort of said to you, "What’s  all this about?" or....

GEORGE: No not really.

ANNA: And...did you think  it was at all funny, or spooky, or.....odd? Or whatever?

GEORGE: You mean weird?

ANNA & KAREN: Or weird.

(Long Pause)

GEORGE: Don’t  know. I thought  it was a bit strange, because...especially where you had the one over the staircase  there, ‘cos  as soon as I saw it I could visualise the old girls coming, you know, with their bingo books and pens.....and just as they  get to the staircase, they go "Oh!" and - CRASH! (all laugh) It’s bound to happen.....

KAREN: (laughs) That ‘d be awful! 

ANNA: That would be awful - I didn’t  really  think of that, but it is right at the top of  the stairs, isn’t it?

GEORGE: And it’s not  there when they’re  approaching  it, maybe - it might come  on just as they get there.

(All chuckle)


ANNA: God.....

GEORGE: They’ll be sueing Mecca, not  you.

KAREN: And....erm.....you must have some little stories and  anechdotes about people that we can put in the booklet....and can we publish it? Not too scandalous!

GEORGE: Ah, they’re mostly scandalous.....!

ANNA: What about......oh, no there was a good story about.....who was telling us that story about  a caller?  About a scam, here.....do know about that?

KAREN: Oh, that was Dave Neale  who was telling  me about  that .

ANNA: It was years ago, wasn’t it? Years and years and years ago......

KAREN: No, it was about Ricky that used to call at Beacon....who went from here to Beacon......

GEORGE: He didn’t go to the Beacon did he?

KAREN: Well, I don’t know.....it was a bloke called Ricky, always wore a dicky bow.

GEORGE: That wasn’t Ricky Warwick. Well, we all used to wear them, till they relaxed things. Erm....Ricky Warwick’s in Spain, I think, ‘cos he bought  some property out there, you know  when it was going cheap?  Years ago.....and er....I heard  he was a bit dicey.

ANNA: Have there been any scams?  Or is that......can you not divulge?

GEORGE: No, it’s bad for business, innit, mainly. They  don’t  like it. That’s why they usually  sack ‘em and don’t  nick ‘em if they catch  somebody.

ANNA: Really?

GEORGE: But...it’s very unlikely  now, with the computers and everything.  Before,  when it was the ball machine, even then it hard to do. But what I heard - and it was Ricky Warwick - he used to call here, this was his base...and then he’d go to Shepherds Bush once  or twice a week to call  sessions, and maybe somewhere  else, maybe  Camden Town.....I forget the other  place. But then, this girl I was going with, her mother  always used to play Bingo at Shepherds Bush. And one time, she happened to be...she had nothing  to do , so she was with her mother  in Shepherds Bush Bingo. Ricky Warwick was calling.....and the way they  do it, they get to the last Full House of the main session - not a Link, because with the Link the numbers are recorded , so - you know, the number of winners and the ticket  numbers  - everything is kept. So....it would be a house game, like, say for about  five hundred pound, which is, going back a few years was alot  of money. And er...if there was only one winner on that  last page - which there  invariably  was - soon as somebody  went  "HOUSE!" ......you know what they’re  like: vwoom! They all dash for the doors! "Wait  for the check."....and they  ignore  it completely.   And..they used to have little cameras , in the hall, in a little cabinet- thing, and  you put the ticket  up against  the camera, and it comes onto the screens......

ANNA: Oh....I’ve heard about that....

GEORGE: .....a visual picture of all  your markings.......

ANNA: Like a video?

GEORGE: ......so if you’d blotted them all out, you couldn’t  even read it, which was a big minus.

ANNA: Oh, so you held up your Bingo book to the camera?

GEORGE: No, the steward would. So, there’s a camera inside  the box , and the image goes on the moniters......and then he’d say "Second check"-  and by then the place’d be near enough empty , ‘cos they’re:"Ah, it’s a winner"- "Is there  a second  check?"....."Yes, there’s a second check"....and he’d put  the.....

(CUSTOMER interrupts, wanting change for slot machines. The change  dispenser  is broken, GEORGE goes to find staff to sort out  the problem, then returns)

ANNA: So what about  the end of this story?

GEORGE: Oh, right...

ANNA: I’m sort of sitting here  waiting to find out how they...

GEORGE: Ah well, the way  you do it is: you got to have a plant. Ricky’s friend...I’m presuming  all this ‘cos it’s only  hearsay, I wasn’t there......

ANNA& KAREN: Yes...yes....

GEORGE: ....and, er, he waits and has a look around  and then gives it to a steward.... now, the steward ‘s got to be in on it as well, and Ricky and the plant, so there’s only three of ‘em. The manager knows  nothing  about  it, nobody else knows anything about  it . The punter is a stranger - you don’t want somebody  who they know is Ricky’s friend, or, you know....and they say "Second  check. Yeah, that’s good as well" ..........and it’s the same ticket gone back  in.

ANNA: What  do you mean? Well, it would be the same ticket, wouldn’t  it?

GEORGE: There  isn’t a second check. They  check  the same  one twice. Okay?  So, the bloke hasn’t  got a winning claim.

KAREN: So, there’s one from the game before, you mean?

GEORGE: No.

KAREN: No.

GEORGE: Say you’ve won, right? And I’m saying I’ve won as well....

KAREN: Oh, I see!

ANNA: Oh, right...right....

KAREN: They split  it....

GEORGE: .....the steward’s checked yours, throws mine  on the floor, then checks yours again.....

KAREN: Right, okay....

GEORGE: ...and I get half of the five hundred!

KAREN: Easy!

GEORGE: And as it happened this time, she’d heard about  him doing strokes like that - she was a bit suspicious - and her mum ‘d called up fairly quickly.  The quicker  you call up the  more  you expect  to get  it on your own, you know : if it goes to sixty numbers, you know you’ve probably got  to share the win, right?  So, this was, I don’t know, probably  on forty-odd, or something.....Full House...."Oh, you’ve got  it on your own, Mum" ...and all this, and  all of a sudden, "Second  check!". So she says,"Where’s  the second  cheque  come  from?" She did have a mouth on ‘er.....and he says,"Ohh.....second cheque."- he was like that, Ricky...a bit bossy , didn’t want to know, like -  so, she says "I want to see both winning claims!". By then the place was nearly  empty, Ricky Warwick was standing there saying, "Stop making a nuisance!", trying to brush ‘er off, and all of a sudden there’s a lot of shouting and that, and the manager’s  appeared.  So, the manager’s  there to sort it out, sort of thing, her mother’s  got the money , and....Ricky Warwick’s been asked not to come back to Shepherds Bush. But he didn’t get the sack from here. That was the funny thing.

ANNA: That’s quite a good story.

GEORGE: See, if somebody’s  playing on the wrong coloured page here, and you call up on the right colour, and I check yours - you know it gives you an image of your claim....it don’t matter what you’ve done to your claim , it’ll be all brand new on my screen ‘cos it’s not the actual ticket  - and then if the other person on the wrong page claims  as well, I enter their number - I don’t hear the colour of the page, or the steward doesn’t say it or something - and it says "Already  checked.....Already checked"....you’ve seen that flash?

ANNA: What does it say?

GEORGE: "Already  checked". And of course I know it can’t  already  ‘ve been checked because I’d have had it from a different person. So I’ve to check the colour  again and the ticket, and one of ‘em will be maybe  two pages at once, or something. So, you can’t put  a second check in, because  that would flash "Already  checked".

KAREN: So it’s a lot harder now, now it’s all computerised?

GEORGE: There was another  way, where there was a manager.......where was it? Camberwell.  I was at a club in Camberwell, and right behind us was a little  club called  The Star - it was a right  dump  - and they only used to get in there people what had the needle with our club, ‘cos our club was big, and  they were a little scruffy club...

KAREN: I think I’ve been in that one actually. It’s changed names so many  times, it was "Jasmines"  for a bit, then it was "Zetters"....is that the one?

GEORGE: Yeah, yeah, that was the big one. That was the old MECCA.

KAREN: Oh, was it?

GEORGE: Before Rank bought MECCA, and then Jasmine bought  it off of Rank ...

KAREN: Right, and now it’s Zetters....

GEORGE: ....now they’ve sold it to Zetters. But the one right behind it - I think it’s been pulled down now - there was a little  one, used to be....

ANNA: Where was it? Between Camberwell and Peckham?

KAREN: It’s on the Walworth road, I think....

GEORGE: I  don’t know Camberwell very well. I just used to go there and call and then come back. I would  call at Camberwell, near Camberwell Green.

ANNA: I’ve got a friend who made  a film in a Bingo club in Camberwell...

KAREN: Really?

ANNA: Yeah....with the Bingo players . That must be the one.

GEORGE: Oh, that’s probably  the one. Anyway, this little one behind it....a manager I used to know who left the company...we heard he’d got a job there......erm....oh, I can tell you who it is, ‘cos he committed suicide  some time ago, so he’s dead and gone.......Georgie Bean. He only had one leg - he had a plastic  leg - and he was a right comedian and entertainer-type-of-bloke.......

ANNA: He was the manager?

GEORGE: He was the manager for MECCA, or assistant manager, and he left the company....and then it turns up he’d got this job in The Star. He says, "I’m your competition round the back ‘ere!". So, me and the manager, Barry, who I was friends with, we went round to see him to say, "How’re you doin’?", like....we thought he can’t be getting  tuppence for being in charge of this place.  And he had cigars on the table and drink and everything, and he’s, "Ooh, lovely!" he says, "This is paradise, is this club!"...grotty little place, it was empty - there was no punters there  then, ‘cos it was teatime, you know. So, Barry’s saying,"Go on, how d’you get money  outta this place then?"  And he said........you know when you get  rejects ? Say, this is the starting number, this is the finished number of books.....so many sold, less rejects,say we used three, six, and so on and so on......you take the six rejected books - only odd ones or twos that haven’t been sold - you can’t  sell ‘em later ‘cos the serial number’ll be out of sequence, so you have to reject  those - there’s usually  a little  handful or something - and, at our club, round the corner  from him, we used to keep the month’s rejects  in a shoebox. And that was nowhere  near full up , that was a month’s.....’cos there’s only a couple each session, see. Sometimes  there’s none - we used to have to dash around going "Who wants to get lucky  on some books?" an’ all that nonsense, just to get rid of ‘em all.....score brownie points with Head Office, sort of thing.  So this bloke, old Georgie Bean....we said, "er...how’re you making money  ‘ere then?" He said,"Ave another scotch.....‘ave another cigar !" We"re  going, "Phwaw! " you know!  (All Laugh) He says, "You know them rejects you keep in a shoebox?"  We said, "Yeah?".  He said,"D’you want to see  last weeks rejects of mine?  They’re  there."  And there was all these brown....er, black rubbish sacks. And we said "You can"t  have that many ." He says, "Nah, there’s not  that many," he says, "but there’s newspapers, and teabags, and all the sort of crap I can find all stuffed and slimed up in these bags. So, if the Gaming Board walk in, and want to see my rejects.....sort’em out - they’re  in there somewhere!"

ANNA: Can you throw them away?  Or are you meant  to return them?

GEORGE: You must have them on the premises  for a certain amount of time. In a small club, you probably  don’t have to send them to Head Office or anything, you know, you can just keep them in sacks of rubbish. Then if the Gaming Board  ever did bother  to call on this grotty  little place , they wouldn’t want to sort through all that...and, according to him, there was hundreds and hundreds of  rejected books, that didn’t really exist. So they’d been sold, and he’d had the money, so it didn’t go on the prize money  board........see how it works? It was the simplest  thing....he was rolling in it. But he left there...I don’t know what happenened there, but anyway....

ANNA: How do you know he committed suicide?

GEORGE: He was erm......he had this girlfriend he always lived with, and ‘cos I suppose it must be awkward if you’ve only got one leg, sort of.....getting a chat up line ....(All Laugh)...old Hoppy.......it’d be hard getting  a new bird....so he was really attached to this bird  even though they weren’t  married.....he was really attached to this bird, that he....like....he lived for her, sort of thing. And er...she dumped him. A few weeks later we heard he’d killed  himself.
But nowadays  I don’t think there’s as much skullduggery  and that.

ANNA : Right... it’s been tightened up.  New technology,  it does change things doesn’t  it?  It takes away all those little gaps and areas where you can make things up and.....

KAREN: Yeah, but I’m sure there are little gaps where you can find......they’re just different.

GEORGE: Oh yeah, you’ll always find more....we had them come  round one time .....I knew the manager  was at  it , but I didn’t know what he was doing, I couldn’t suss how he was doing  it....because he’d go down the road, and he says, "Coming down the betting shop, George?" and I’d say "Ahh  no....I’m fed up with it." and he"d say "Oh, you can’t  lose if you bet like this!" . So he’d go down there  and he’d have, like, fifty pound on the favourite or something, and then he’d sort out another one. If that lost, "How much  have I got to have to get my money  back and show a profit? ...er....seventy pound.....so, seventy pound on the next  favourite .....and he says,"Do you want to be in on this?" I says, "I can’t  afford all that !" ‘Cos that was a lot of money  in them days . I suppose wages were about  fifty quid a week, and he was betting hundreds  a day, you know. Fancy betting hundreds a day, like!  And he said, erm..."They turn up, and I get all my money back and the profit." And I said,"Yeah, but, that’s as old as the hills", you know....you’ve got to lose. ‘Cos one day, it’s only got to have ten losers in a row, and it’ll knock you....so.....and it can go fifty losers in a row, I’ve seen it go fifty losers -  favourites  - nothing unusual.....so he was doing this, he wouldn’t  have it. Anyway, in the end he didn’t turn up one day , and there was a bit of pandaemonium, ‘ cos we were all trying to unlock  the offices and things without  keys. And all of a sudden, this team arrived from Head Office, and er...he’d just absconded, sort of thing . He’d got so far in debt somehow  that he’d absconded.  So, the petty cash  in the office - we used to keep in offices in them days - that was all gone. And the weird thing.....they went around all the cigarette machines....they used to have them wooden cigarette machines on the walls......and....they had no money  in ‘em, and all the cigarette  packets  were empty packets!

(All laugh)

KAREN: He’d been selling cigarettes as well.....!

GEORGE: You know, he’d been putting empty  packets  in place of.....so they thought,"Oh, the machine’s  full up, there won’t be any money  to collect" but  it was full up with empty  packets !

ANNA: And that was that - gone, never seen nor heard of again?

GEORGE: There was another one....I don’t know how he didn’t  get  in the papers  for it. He came to the Carlton Rooms once - I was there  donkeys  years  ago - he appeared there once, "visiting management " or something, and he thought  he was a showman, you know. Fat bloke, only about  twenty-two  or something like that I suppose. And he’d say,"Hallo George!" and all this.  Now I don’t  know him from Adam, and I don’t really like all that kind of thing. "I’ll call the start of this session, just for a few games, get me hand  in......Good evening Ladies and Gents! I’m so-and-so...!" And all this lark.....and they went (groan) - you know how some of them are - and he’s  going on, all bombastic, "I’ll leave the rest to George - go on, George...." he’d swank about the hall, having a drink and that. I thought, "what  sort of man’s this?" And he had this...I don’t know if it was his wife, or girlfriend, always on his shoulder, like, wherever he went. And, anyway, saw no more  of him, and then we found out there was....MECCA clubs - that’s the old MECCA, we had over a hundred clubs, we were the biggest in Bingo - there wereMecca clubs being held up at gunpoint  by two or three people. Anyway, they sussed out  in the end  somehow  it was this bloke, and he was doing armed holdups - he was a nutcase, he must’ve seen Bonnie and Clyde or something and gone berserk!   And...he’d know the layout  of the club , and then he started doing clubs where they knew him ! They knew his face!

KAREN: NO ! Didn’t  he try and disguise himself? Put a stocking over his head?

GEORGE: No.

ANNA: That’s amazing!  He must’ve been living out a fantasy.....

KAREN: ...Indestructible....

GEORGE: Yeah. I think he thought  he’d already been sussed, so he thought  "Bugger it. Why cover up if I’ve been sussed?"  and he was just doing it , going from club  to club, and the alarm was out , you know,"If you see so-and-so..." and there was a picture of him, "....call the police  immediately, and don’t ...er...tackle ‘im in any way, ‘cos he carries an armed gun at all times."

ANNA: So when was that? How many years ago was that?

GEORGE: Ohh, years ago now. Fifteen or twenty. He went  down...I forget how long....he went to prison. Raving mad! 

ANNA: Robbing Bingo halls! How much money do you think....? I suppose there  is quite a lot of money  isn’t there, on the premises  in a day?

(GEORGE laughs ) I mean, not  the content, but....like.....standing on a podium......

ANNA: Or belief. I mean you have to believe to play, don’t you? You have to believe  in luck.

GEORGE: You have to....if you heard about  Bingo from somebody, who said "Ooh, we played Bingo, had a few  drinks and a laugh, and  so-and-so won a hundred  pound or something, you’d think "Oh...not a bad night out", you’d come along. Something goes wrong, or you get some drunken idiot, or there’s a fight, that sort of thing. It’s like in gambling clubs, if you get a fight, it kills the atmosphere, and the takings go down. The way I tell is, count  the teatime  books, compared to the early, and the late session books compared to the early.....and they should be... ‘cos, you play too much Bingo now. ANNA: Why?

KAREN: Just not many  people....?

GEORGE: Eighty people.....which isn’t bad if it’s a tiny club, but eighty are lost here, you know. You’re playing for bits and pieces, and you get no atmosphere  at all that way.....they just think "Ooh...what ‘re we doing  here?" . But they’ll come back the following Wednesday, see?  People who moan, openly, they’ll always come back.  If they’re not moaning then they’re  upset, and they’re  probably  thinking "Last time I’m in this place."But when they say "Blah, blah, blah.......prize money’s wrong......Blah, blah, blah......"- they’ll be there the next week saying the same bloody  thing, so you don’t worry about it.

ANNA: It’s like that fish and chip shop we went in, and we both said we’d never go back there because they were so rude. But if we’d lived round the corner we’d probably have said something. They wouldn’t  let us sit down and have a tea, and there were,you know, twenty empty tables!

KAREN: Yeah, there was no-one else in there....

ANNA: It was really stupid. We were both talking about  how......in a way about what  you’re saying - like, when you’re really angry with something, you don’t  want to communicate, you don’t want to waste your time on it, and you just go.

GEORGE: You just don’t  come back.

KAREN: It’s strange. I mean, why do they? Why do they just keep coming back?
