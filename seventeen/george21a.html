GEORGE: ....there was no punters there then,'cos it was teatime, you know. So, Barry's saying,"Go on, how d'you get money outta this place then?" And he said........you know when you get rejects ? Say, this is the starting number, this is the finished number of books.....so many sold, less rejects,say we used three, six, and so on and so on......you take the six rejected books - only odd ones or twos that haven't been sold - you can't sell'em later'cos the serial number'll be out of sequence, so you have to reject those - there's usually a little handful or something - and, at our club, round the corner from him, we used to keep the month's rejects in a shoebox. And that was nowhere near full up , that was a month's.....'cos there's only a couple each session, see. Sometimes there's none - we used to have to dash around going "Who wants to get lucky on some books?" an' all that nonsense, just to get rid of'em all.....score brownie points with Head Office, sort of thing. So this bloke, old Georgie Bean....we said, "er... how're you making money 'ere then?" He said,"Ave another scotch.....'ave another cigar !" We"re going, "Phwaw! " you know! (All Laugh) He says, "You know them rejects you keep in a shoebox?" We said, "Yeah?". He said, "D'you want to see last weeks rejects of mine? They're there." And there was all these brown....er, black rubbish sacks. And we said "You can"t have that many ." He says, "Nah, there's not that many," he says, "but there's newspapers, and teabags, and all the sort of crap I can find all stuffed and slimed up in these bags. So, if the Gaming Board walk in, and want to see my rejects.....sort'em out - they're in there somewhere!"
