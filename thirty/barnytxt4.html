At one with the commercial and social purpose of the place Sinclair manned the stall for the day selling to those that would buy and talking to those that would talk.

In Kilburn across London a similarly unassuming series of interventions was taking place at the Mecca State Bingo Club. For Anna Best these were just one part of nearly a years work with Camden Arts Centre, looking at the experiences of Bingo devotees and exploring the strange parallels between gambling and religion. Where Sinclair's market stall was a consciously finite affair, Best sees her work at Mecca as 'a long slow trickle' . As an artist most comfortable with the techniques of the researcher, it comes as a suprise that Best's real aims for the project were inherently supernatural, 'I wanted' she confesses 'to perform a miracle' .

The project began with a consultation period, during which short interviews with regular bingo players at Mecca were conducted by Best and her key collaborator Karen Stripp. The two jokingly became known by staff at the bingo hall as 'the therapists'. This period also saw regular discussions at Camden, involving amongst others the designer Thomas Andersson and Mecca's customer services manager, Shaun Fox. At both venues these were ideas sessions. At Mecca, players confided in the artists about their enjoyment of the game, their use of lucky charms, and in some cases, more sinisterly, about their addiction to gambling. At the Arts Centre too, talk wandered to people's personal encounters with luck, games, faith and rituals.
