From the period of consultation came the structure for an event and the month of June was chosen for its numerical symmetry; thirty days, thirty numbers. Best chose specific images suggested by the interviews she had conducted and projected them daily in different locations around the Mecca building. Special Bingo events took place in Camden and Kilburn, including at one point the reversion of the Mecca building to its original purpose as a cinema. A short film interweaving Best's gathered images was accompanied by music played on the halls original Wurlitzer organ. For Best the project is not yet over, she is yet to perform the miracle she was hoping for and at present she is writing up this whole period of 'research' to form a publication.

With all public projects it is interesting to trace a line of agendas back from the artists to the curator, from the curator to the institution and from the institution to the funding body. Besides noting what a long distance it is from the policy-makers to these 'new audiences' they make policies for, it is also important to ask who in this line-up is listening to whom, and how the hopes for such initiatives at their conception in the boardroom differ from those at their delivery by the artist.

Unusually for a public art project,'I love Real Life' was initiated by a commercial gallery, The Agency in Shoreditch. While planning a show of Sinclair's work at the gallery, the directors saw the opportunity to present a broader view of his practice and provide some publicity for both the show and the gallery.
