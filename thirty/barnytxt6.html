Sinclair was interested in placing art in a 'real' environment and seeing how it might function, while Dalston town centre manager Joanne Rogers saw that the event would bring new visitors to Ridley Road Market and provide something of interest for regular market-goers. With her support the project received funding through a local SRB grant.

The North London Link Project, of which Anna Best's work with Mecca is just one constituent, is funded through the Camden Arts Centre by Lottery money and the Henry Moore Foundation. While the funding bodies are talking about 'greatly benefiting the public', the institution is talking about the 'potential for new audiences', the project coordinator is talking about 'trusting the artist' and the artist herself is adamant: 'I don't want to tailor something to a policy, this is my work' .

Somewhere between the beginning and end of these chains of influence there is a none too subtle change of agenda, from a picture of the artist as educator, do-gooder and ambassador for all things creative, to one of the artist as researcher, free agent, and ground-breaking subversive. The relationship that the latter picture has to wider audiences is notably less didactic, and more egalitarian. These artists require the assistance of a varied public to understand their own work, and not vice-versa.
