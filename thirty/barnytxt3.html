'The tattoo reflects a lifetime's commitment to a project which aims to engage as wide an audience as possible in a debate surrounding the interesting gaps between life as it is lived by everyday people, and the aspirations afforded by spectacular life as we understand it through mediated images of the world.'

At six o clock on the morning of June the 26th, the tattoo and Sinclair attached to it were on their way to Ridley Rd. Market In Dalston, Northeast London, where the regular traders were setting up their stalls for a hot and busy Saturday. Ridley Rd is one of London's largest and most multicultural street markets with stalls selling everything from fresh fruit and vegetables to musical greetings cards, boiler chickens and sari fabrics.

It was in this tangibly 'authentic' environment that Sinclair chose to launch his own brand of 'I love Real Life' products. No stranger to building and performing in bizarre structures Sinclair spent the morning setting up a specially customised market stall, complete with a large red neon sign, hand painted banners, and brightly coloured boxes on which he displayed his product range. Coffee mugs, T-shirts, key rings, ballpens, badges and caps were available, all emblazoned with the logo 'I love Real Life'. By the time the first shoppers arrived he had all but disappeared into the hubbub and colour of the market, marked apart only by the tireless and surreal repetition of his message and the strange dysfunctional nature of his product.
