There are many kinds of artists working in the public sphere, just as there are many kinds of public art, what remains pertinent is to what extent the term 'commission' signals 'compromise' for artists who have their own reasons for working with non-art audiences. Peter Cross, coordinator of the London Link Project is quick to point out that with live and research based artwork like that of Sinclair and Best, this question of compromise is amplified. Where art-objects placed in public have a kind of 'like it or not' infallibility, art processes in the public sphere are a far riskier business. Best states this emphatically in relation to her work at Mecca:

'The artist in these kind of projects is in the 'front line', you can't do that without taking absolute responsibility for what you are doing, it has to be your work.'

It is particularly notable that organisations who support more experimental interventions into the public space are going out on a limb to accommodate projects that offer none of the safety and familiarity of the by now ubiquitous exhibition / interpretation / education approach. Those working at The Agency and The Camden Arts Centre have had to show an often uncomfortable flexibility to organise projects like those in Dalston and Kilburn within their usual programme of on-site exhibitions.
