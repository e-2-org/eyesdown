A week of small miracles

week of small miracles

1. Salt rolling grain by grain along the slate table top in the cafe, racing with grains of salt.

2. Sister Marie faxing me and sending an easter present.

3. When I was first on the phone to Sister Marie I said 'oh my god', the phone line cut and a machine voice said 'please hold the line we are trying to connect you', then Sister Marie came back and said her pet cat had sat on the phone...

4. When I told a friend I'd got a fax from Sister Marie my bike light suddenly came on and started flashing. Earlier tha day had been trying to get the xmas lights to work and Karen going near them and they just came on.

5. Cycling along and dropped gloves on Elgin Ave (after buying a choclate bar), retracing steps and met a man on a bike carrying my gloves in his hand. Cycled up to him and exchanged, he said 'you've been lucky' and cycled off.

6. Coming home from Camden cycling along Baker Street. I saw a shooting star, extraordinary, not a normal star, like a cartoon or disney-star with a tail, flying from left to right across Baker Street just above the roof tops. Stopped and waited for more, perhaps a firework, but saw nothing else. It was supernatural, too close and too slow motion to be real.

7. A friend from twenty years before called Zophia walking in to Camden out of the blue, while I'm researching Sophia Richmond aka Sister Marie. Both are of Polish origin.

Miracles

Miracles

A miracle, philosophically speaking, is never a mere coincidence no matter how extraordinary or significant. (If you miss a plane and the plane crashes, that is not a miracle unless God intervened in the natural course of events causing you to miss the flight.) A miracle is a supernaturally (divinely) caused event - an event (ordinarily) different from what would have occurred in the normal ("natural") course of events. It is a divine overriding of, or interference with,the natural order. As such, it need not be extraordinary, marvelous or significant, and it must be something other than a coincidence, no matter how remarkable - unless the "coincidence" itself is caused by divine intervention (i.e., not really a coincidence at all). Miracles, however, are ordinarily understood to be not just products of divine intervention in the natural order, but extraordinary, marvelous and significant as well. Thus, Aquinas says a miracle is "beyond the order commonly observed;" and Dr. Eric Mascall says that the word "miracle" "signifies in Christian theology a striking interposition of divine power by which the operations of the ordinary course of nature are overruled, suspended, or modified.
